## 5. mandatory lead qualification

A business only counts toward the target of 20 if it passes the following checks.

### A. website opportunity

Our primary target is a business with no dedicated, functioning website of its own.

For each prospect:

1. Search the exact business name with its city.
2. Search the business phone number, owner name if known, and public email.
3. Examine its Google Business Profile and official Facebook, Instagram, Nextdoor, and other public profiles.
4. Check whether those profiles link to a dedicated website.
5. Open and inspect any possible website that appears in search results.

A missing website field in Clay or a directory is not sufficient evidence that the business has no website.

A Facebook page, directory listing, or third-party booking page does not necessarily mean that a business has its own dedicated website.

If the business already has a website, only consider it as a separate upgrade prospect when you can document a meaningful, specific improvement opportunity. Label these prospects clearly as WEBSITE UPGRADE rather than NO WEBSITE.

Prioritize NO WEBSITE prospects.

### B. Google reviews and rating

The target qualification is:

- Google rating of at least 4.0 stars.
- Between 10 and 250 Google reviews, inclusive.

Verify these figures against the actual Google Business Profile or another reliable source that clearly identifies the Google listing.

Do not substitute ratings or review counts from Thumbtack, Yelp, Facebook, Nextdoor, or another platform for Google metrics.

Do not count placeholder values such as zero or an unknown rating as verified data.

If either metric cannot be verified, label the prospect UNVERIFIED and do not count it among the 20 qualified leads.

### C. recent business activity

Look for clear evidence of activity within the past six months.

Examples include a dated social media post, a recent business update, a recent review, or an up-to-date service announcement.

Record the actual date and the source URL.

Do not invent a last-active date or infer recent activity from a profile that merely exists.

### D. email address

A publicly listed, usable business email address is mandatory.

Accept an email address found on:

- The business's own website.
- An official business social media page.
- A verified public business profile.
- A reliable public business directory that clearly identifies the same business.

Open the source and check that the email actually belongs to the intended business whenever possible.

Never construct or guess an email address from the business name or domain.

A search snippet alone is insufficient when the identity of the business or email address is uncertain.

If no trustworthy public business email can be found, reject the prospect for this email campaign. Do not count a phone-only contact as an email-ready lead.

### E. correct business identity

Verify that the name, contact details, location, services, and website status refer to the same business.

Reject records where the phone number belongs to another company, where a business in a different city has been conflated with the prospect, or where several unrelated companies share a similar name.

## 7. the email-writing format

Every email should be written specifically for the individual business.

Target approximately 100–170 words, excluding the compliance footer.

### Subject line

Use a short, relevant, non-deceptive subject line.

Examples:

- A website idea for [Business Name]
- Helping more local customers find [Business Name]
- An idea for [Business Name]'s online presence

Do not use fake reply subjects, false urgency, misleading claims, or exaggerated promises.

### Greeting

Use the owner's first name when it has been verified.

If the owner's name is unknown, use a suitable greeting such as "Hi [Business Name] team,". Never invent a person's name.

### Opening paragraph

Start with one specific, verified detail about the business.

Examples might include a recently advertised service, a distinctive service offering, an actual project, or a clearly documented business initiative.

Do not write generic openings like "I am impressed by your outstanding business" unless you have a genuine factual reason for saying so.

### Website opportunity

Explain one or two concrete ways a website could benefit that specific business.

Examples:

- Displaying services, prices, and service areas.
- Showing before-and-after project photos.
- Making quote requests easier.
- Providing booking information.
- Answering common customer questions.
- Showcasing qualifications and completed work.

Match the suggestion to the actual business. A handyman might benefit from a project gallery, while a pet groomer might benefit from service information and booking links.

Do not insult the business's existing online presence.

Do not claim its website is broken, outdated, or nonexistent unless that has been verified.

### Introduction

Use this wording as the starting point:

"My name is Andrei, and I'm a web developer who builds websites for small businesses."

Vary the surrounding language naturally, but do not fabricate credentials, client results, or experience.

### Portfolio links

Include both links as plain, clickable URLs:

Portfolio:
https://web-dev-portfolio-swart.vercel.app/

GitHub:
https://github.com/Andiewitz

### Call to action

End with one simple, low-pressure question, tailored to the business.

For example:

"Would you be open to a quick conversation about what a website for [Business Name] could look like?"

Avoid demanding a meeting or pushing for an immediate purchase.

### Sign-off

Best,
Andrei

### Originality requirement

Do not send 20 copies of the exact same email with business names swapped.

Use the verified facts to create genuinely different openings, benefits, and suggestions. Keep the overall structure consistent, but personalize the substance.

## 9. sending requirements

The desired outcome is real outreach to new qualified prospects, not merely a list of drafts.

If an authenticated email integration is available, and all necessary sender details and permissions have been configured, prepare personalized one-to-one emails for qualified businesses.

Send only when the sending workflow is authorized and the applicable commercial-email requirements can be met.

If you cannot actually send emails, prepare the drafts and clearly state that they have not been sent. Never claim an email was sent without confirmation from the sending system.

Before sending commercial emails to U.S. businesses, follow the FTC's CAN-SPAM requirements:

https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business

In particular:

- Use accurate sender and reply information.
- Do not use deceptive subject lines.
- Clearly identify the commercial nature of the outreach.
- Include a valid physical postal mailing address for the sender.
- Provide a clear and functional way to opt out of future marketing messages.
- Honor opt-out requests and maintain a suppression list.
- Do not send further marketing messages to anyone who has asked not to be contacted.

My valid physical postal address and any required sending configuration have not been supplied in this brief. **Do not invent an address or send emails with a placeholder compliance footer.** If these details are missing, continue researching and drafting, but flag the sending step as blocked until the required information is provided and configured.

Do not use misleading tracking, deceptive attachments, fake referrals, or fabricated claims.

Send in a controlled, measured manner rather than blasting a large list in one operation.

## 11. deduplication and campaign tracking

Maintain a persistent lead ledger.

Before researching or contacting a prospect, check it against:

- The permanent exclusion list above.
- All previously researched businesses.
- All previously drafted leads.
- All businesses already contacted.
- All replied, declined, opted-out, or bounced email addresses.
- Businesses with matching phone numbers, owners, domains, or public profiles.

If a business has already been contacted, do not contact it again simply because a new source reveals another email address.

Track these stages separately:

- Researched.
- Qualified.
- Draft ready.
- Sending blocked by configuration.
- Sent.
- Replied.
- Interested.
- Meeting booked.
- Proposal sent.
- Won.
- Lost.
- Opted out.

Do not report drafts as sent emails or sent emails as qualified prospects without the necessary evidence.
